# PhishGuard: Methodology and Evaluation

## 1. Project Overview

PhishGuard is a practical phishing awareness project designed to help users recognize common phishing indicators and develop safer responses to suspicious digital communications.

The project combines educational content, practical exercises, a quick-reference response card, a knowledge quiz, and a pre/post awareness assessment.

The project follows a user-centered approach. Rather than focusing only on the technical characteristics of phishing attacks, PhishGuard focuses on helping users understand the decisions they should make when confronted with suspicious communications.

---

# 2. Project Methodology

The project was developed through six main stages.

## Stage 1 — Research

Information was reviewed from reputable cybersecurity and consumer-protection sources to identify:

* Common phishing techniques
* Common warning signs
* Social engineering tactics
* Safe responses to suspicious messages
* Recommended reporting practices
* Account protection measures

The research was then converted into language suitable for users with different levels of technical knowledge.

---

## Stage 2 — Awareness Content Development

The research findings were converted into the PhishGuard Awareness Guide.

The guide explains:

* What phishing is
* Why phishing works
* Common types of phishing
* Warning signs
* Social engineering tactics
* Safe verification methods
* Appropriate responses
* Account protection practices

The content was designed to be practical rather than overly technical.

---

## Stage 3 — Practical Learning Materials

Additional materials were developed to reinforce the information in the awareness guide.

These include:

### Spot-the-Phish Exercise

Participants analyze fictional messages and identify suspicious characteristics.

### Phishing Response Quick Card

A concise reference tool based on:

**STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT**

### Awareness Poster

A visual reminder of the most important phishing warning signs and safe practices.

---

## Stage 4 — Knowledge Assessment

A 10-question phishing awareness quiz was developed to test participants' understanding of:

* Phishing warning signs
* Suspicious links
* Sensitive information
* Verification codes
* Unexpected attachments
* Social engineering tactics
* Reporting procedures

Each question has one correct answer.

---

## Stage 5 — Pre/Post Awareness Assessment

A pre/post assessment approach was developed to evaluate changes in participants' awareness.

Participants may complete the assessment before reviewing the PhishGuard materials and again after completing the awareness activity.

The assessment measures:

* Perceived phishing awareness
* Confidence in identifying suspicious messages
* Understanding of phishing indicators
* Knowledge of safe responses
* Understanding of reporting procedures

Results should be analyzed using anonymous participant identifiers where possible.

---

# 3. Evaluation Framework

The success of PhishGuard can be evaluated using several indicators.

## Awareness Improvement

Compare the average pre-training and post-training assessment scores.

An increase in the post-training score may indicate improved understanding following exposure to the awareness materials.

---

## Phishing Identification

Participants can be evaluated using the Spot-the-Phish exercise.

The percentage of correctly identified suspicious scenarios can be recorded.

---

## Knowledge

The phishing awareness quiz provides a numerical measure of participants' understanding.

Scores can be grouped into:

* Excellent
* Good
* Needs Improvement
* Awareness Gap

---

## Confidence

The pre/post assessment measures participants' confidence in identifying and responding to phishing.

This helps determine whether participants feel better prepared after completing the awareness activity.

---

# 4. Success Criteria

PhishGuard will be considered successful if it achieves the following:

1. A complete phishing awareness guide is produced.
2. Users can identify common phishing warning signs.
3. Users can distinguish suspicious fictional scenarios from safer communications.
4. Users understand how to respond to suspicious messages.
5. Users understand that passwords and verification codes should not be shared through unexpected requests.
6. Users know how to independently verify suspicious requests.
7. Users understand how and where to report phishing.
8. The project provides a method for measuring awareness improvement.
9. The final awareness materials are understandable to non-technical users.

# 6. Data Analysis

The project can compare:

* Average pre-training score
* Average post-training score
* Average knowledge score
* Percentage of correctly identified phishing scenarios
* Changes in self-reported confidence

A simple improvement calculation can be used:

**Improvement = Post-training score − Pre-training score**

If percentage improvement is required:

**Percentage Improvement = ((Post-training score − Pre-training score) / Pre-training score) × 100**

Only actual collected results should be reported as project findings.

Example values must be clearly identified as examples and must not be presented as real participant results.

---

# 7. Ethical Considerations

PhishGuard is designed as an awareness project rather than an operational phishing campaign.

The project will not:

* Send real phishing messages to unsuspecting individuals.
* Collect passwords.
* Collect verification codes.
* Collect banking information.
* Deploy malicious links.
* Deploy malware.
* Collect unnecessary personal information.

Any phishing examples used in the project are fictional and intended for education.

If participants are involved in assessment activities, participation should be voluntary where appropriate and participants should understand the purpose of the activity.

---

# 8. Privacy Considerations

The project should follow data-minimization principles.

Participant names should not be required when they are unnecessary for evaluation.

Anonymous identifiers such as:

* P001
* P002
* P003

can be used instead.

Results should preferably be presented in aggregate rather than identifying individual participants.

No passwords, financial information, authentication codes, or other sensitive credentials should be requested.

---

# 9. Project Limitations

The project has several limitations.

### Limited Sample Size

If the assessment is conducted with a small number of participants, the results may not represent the wider population.

### Self-Reported Confidence

Participants may report increased confidence without necessarily demonstrating the same level of improvement in real-world situations.

### Educational Scenarios

The Spot-the-Phish exercises use controlled fictional scenarios. Real phishing attacks may use more sophisticated techniques.

### Short Evaluation Period

A short assessment can measure immediate learning but may not demonstrate whether awareness remains effective over a long period.

### Human Behavior

Phishing awareness can reduce risk but cannot guarantee that a person will always recognize or avoid a phishing attempt.

---

# 10. Recommendations

Based on the objectives of the project, the following recommendations are proposed.

## Regular Awareness Training

Organizations should provide phishing awareness training regularly rather than treating it as a one-time activity.

## Reinforce Key Messages

Users should regularly be reminded to:

**STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT**

## Encourage Reporting

Users should have a clear and simple method for reporting suspicious messages.

## Promote Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication should be enabled where available to provide an additional layer of account protection.

## Verify Unusual Requests

Requests involving money, credentials, sensitive information, or unusual account actions should be independently verified.

## Continue Education

Awareness materials should be updated as phishing techniques and communication methods evolve.

# 12. Expected Impact

PhishGuard is intended to improve users' practical understanding of phishing and encourage safer decision-making when handling unexpected digital communications.

The project emphasizes a simple principle:

> **Think before you click.**

By combining education, practical scenarios, quick-reference guidance, and assessment, PhishGuard provides a structured approach to phishing awareness.

---

# 13. Conclusion

Phishing remains an important cybersecurity awareness challenge because attackers can exploit human trust, urgency, fear, curiosity, and authority.

Technical security controls remain important, but users also play an important role in preventing successful social engineering attacks.

PhishGuard addresses this human element by providing accessible educational materials and practical exercises that encourage users to stop, check, verify, and report suspicious communications.

The project's evaluation framework provides a way to measure whether participants demonstrate improved phishing awareness after completing the training.

Ultimately, PhishGuard aims to help users develop safer digital habits and become a stronger layer of defense against phishing attacks.
