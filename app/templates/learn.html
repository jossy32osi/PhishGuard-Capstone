PHISHING AWARENESS

Learn to Recognize Phishing

Phishing attacks use deceptive messages, links, requests, and impersonation to trick people into taking unsafe actions.

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of social engineering in which an attacker attempts to deceive a person into revealing information, clicking a malicious or suspicious link, opening an attachment, sending money, or performing another unsafe action.

Phishing messages can appear to come from trusted organizations, colleagues, banks, schools, delivery services, or technical support.

Common Warning Signs

🚨

Urgency

Messages may pressure you to act immediately or threaten consequences if you do not respond.

🔗

Suspicious Links

Unexpected links may lead to websites designed to imitate legitimate services.

🔐

Sensitive Information

Be cautious when a message asks for passwords, verification codes, payment details, or personal data.

🎭

Impersonation

Attackers may pretend to be managers, colleagues, banks, schools, support teams, or other trusted contacts.

🛡️ Before You Act — Ask Yourself

Use this quick checklist whenever you receive an unexpected message or request.

☐ Do I recognize the sender?
☐ Was I expecting this message?
☐ Is the request urgent or unusual?
☐ Does the link or attachment look trustworthy?
☐ Is the message asking for sensitive information?
☐ Can I verify the request independently?

The PhishGuard Safety Model

01

STOP

Pause. Don't allow urgency or pressure to control your decision.

02

CHECK

Examine the sender, request, links, attachments, and warning signs.

03

VERIFY

Confirm the request through a trusted and independent communication channel.

04

REPORT

Report suspicious messages using the appropriate organizational or institutional procedure.

Remember

A message does not have to look obviously fake to be suspicious. Familiar names, realistic language, and urgent requests can all be used to create trust and pressure.

STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT

When in doubt, don't click.
